## Sermon

{{< collapse summary="**Sermon Summary**">}}

In this sermon, Dr. Jeff Pennington focuses on unity within the church, drawing insights from 1 Corinthians 1:10-17. He emphasizes that unity is a beautiful and pleasant state, comparing it to precious anointing oil and life-giving dew from Psalm 133.

Dr. Pennington addresses the devastating effects of disunity, particularly when it arises from factions and the idolization of individual preachers like Paul, Apollos, or Cephas. He highlights how modern communication, such as emails and social media rants, can fuel these divisions, urging believers to be mindful of how they represent Christ's unity to the world.

The core message is that unity must be built on the foundation of gospel preaching, not eloquent wisdom or personal allegiances. Paul himself thanked God he baptized few in Corinth, ensuring that the focus remained on Christ and His sacrifice, not on the baptizer. The sermon concludes by stressing that the power lies in the message of Jesus, not the preacher, and that true unity comes from centering everything on the gospel of Jesus Christ. The church's strength lies in its generational togetherness and shared faith in the gospel, which is more powerful than any worldly differences.

## Discussion Questions

### Question 1

Pastor Jeff reads from Psalm 133, describing unity as "precious oil" that flows down and "life-giving dew".

* What do these vivid images of oil and dew suggest about the experience and benefits of unity within a community? How is unity portrayed as something essential or enriching?
* In what tangible ways can we contribute to making our fellowship feel as refreshing and nourishing as "life-giving dew"?

### Question 2

The sermon highlights the problem of disunity in the Corinthian church, where people declared loyalties like "I follow Paul," "I follow Apollos," or "I follow Cephas". Pastor Jeff also connects this to modern issues like critical emails and social media rants.

* What are some common ways we see groups or individuals divide themselves today, both inside and outside the church?
* Why is it so tempting for people to form these "divisive loyalties" or use communication in ways that create "factions"? What deeper needs or insecurities might drive this behavior?
* Thinking about our own interactions, both online and offline, what specific "weapons" or habits might we need to lay down to foster greater unity and avoid contributing to division?

### Question 3

Paul asks, "Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?". Pastor Jeff emphasizes that true gospel preaching is "about the glory of Jesus and you being faithful to point as many people as you can to him".

* What is the central point Paul is making through these rhetorical questions?
* How does focusing on Christ crucified and the gospel—rather than human achievements or personalities—serve as the ultimate antidote to division and the source of authentic unity?
* In our conversations and ministries, how can we intentionally ensure that Jesus remains the undisputed "center of our unity," and not any particular person, program, or preference?

### Question 4

Pastor Jeff states that "the unbelieving world needs to see a church that's together and unified", and that when we walk in unity, "the taste of the eternal love of heaven is given not just to us but to the whole world who watches".

* What specific impact does our unity or disunity have on those outside the church, according to the sermon?
* Why is the unity of believers such a powerful and unique witness to a world often characterized by division and conflict? What does it communicate about God?
* What practical steps can we take to ensure our unity is a clear and compelling demonstration of Christ's love?
