## Sermon

{{< collapse summary="**Sermon Summary**">}}

Worship opens as a deliberate act of participation, moving from familiar hymns into a focused reading of 1 Corinthians 2:1–5. The passage highlights a resolute commitment to a single center: Jesus Christ and him crucified. Paul arrived in Corinth without reliance on rhetorical flair or human wisdom; he proclaimed the cross in weakness, fear, and trembling so that faith would rest on God’s power rather than human skill. That posture shaped every ministry decision and kept the gospel central above reputation or eloquence.

Paul’s life illustrates a calling to establish and sustain communities of faith. God called Paul to plant churches across diverse cultures, and Paul invested deeply in those congregations—writing letters, sending representatives, correcting error, and offering care. Corinth became a particular example of a wayward church that squandered spiritual inheritance through division and immoral living. Paul responded with pastoral passion: correction mixed with love, firmness mixed with grief, aiming to restore rather than to shame.

A surprising thread runs through Paul’s account: giftedness did not become ground for pride, and limitations did not become cause for withdrawal. Though highly educated and culturally adept, Paul refused to let strengths eclipse the gospel. At the same time, palpable weaknesses—fear, trembling, and difficulties in speech—did not disqualify him. God’s pattern often involves calling the unready and equipping the willing: God does not call the qualified; God qualifies the called.

Dependence on the Holy Spirit emerges as practical theology. Paul sought and demonstrated the Spirit’s power so the message would not rest on human wisdom but on divine demonstration. Scripture citations reinforce a life led by the Spirit, producing fruit and enabling bold speech in the moment of need. The cross stands as the sufficient center: the gospel alone carries saving power for Jew and Gentile.

The conclusion presses for introspection and response. Believers must name specific callings, cultivate passion for God’s people, refuse to let personal gifts or weaknesses disrupt the gospel, and live by the Spirit’s empowering presence. The assembly moves from teaching into prayerful examination, urging repentance, commitment, and a renewed resolve to make the cross the heartbeat of all ministry.

## Discussion Questions

### Question 1

Paul was an example in living out God’s call. For Paul it was the establishment of churches, what is your specific task that God has called you to do for him? Are you laser focused on that calling?

### Question 2

Are you passionate and joyfully engaged in the lives of your fellow church members? What might be making this difficult?

### Question 3

Paul had strengths and weaknesses and was transparent about them. Do you understand, are you aware, of your spiritual strengths and weaknesses? How do we leverage the strengths to honor the Lord with humble service? How do we minimize our weaknesses? 

### Question 4

Have you entrusted others to speak honestly about your own strengths and weaknesses?

### Question 5

How do you regularly preach the gospel in word and deed?
