## Sermon

{{< collapse summary="**Sermon Summary**">}}

First Corinthians 2:9–3:4 unfolds a sharp contrast between divine wisdom revealed by the Spirit and human limitations that leave many believers immature. Paul first insists that no eye, ear, or human heart can grasp the fullness of what God has prepared, so God gives the Holy Spirit to disclose those hidden thoughts. The Spirit enables true understanding of gospel realities—justification, atonement, and the imputed righteousness of Christ—so that believers can move from mere admiration into life-shaping joy. The text uses the image of a caterpillar turned butterfly to show how regeneration changes identity: the indwelling Spirit frees believers from the small, crawling life and equips them to soar in new obedience and delight.

Paul then maps three spiritual conditions. The “spiritual” person discerns God’s deep wisdom and lives in growing joy. The “natural” or worldly person, devoid of the Spirit, finds spiritual truths foolish and remains captive to a deadly limitation. A third group appears in Corinth: people who are truly in Christ but persist in fleshly, infantile behavior—saved but acting like the lost. This carnality does not mean a loss of salvation or approval to remain unchanged; rather, it signals stalled growth, misplaced priorities, and a need for correction.

Practical application centers on nourishment and transformation. Spiritual maturity requires regular feeding on Scripture and the Spirit’s interpretation; Sunday attendance alone cannot produce sustained maturity. The congregation receives a direct call to repent, to accept Christ if not yet converted, and to leave infantile living for a life shaped by gospel truth in every role—marriage, parenting, work, and community. The passage insists that the Spirit’s work brings both assurance of standing before God and a sanctifying impulse that molds daily behavior. The closing appeal presses for personal response: embrace the Spirit’s revelation, pursue steady growth in the Word, and surrender patterns of fleshly living so the deepening joy of knowing Christ becomes the habitual reality.

## Discussion Questions

### Question 1

The posture of the life of a saint is repentance and the constant attitude for a saint is thanksgiving. Take some time and share the reasons why we are thankful for Christ and the wisdom of God in Him crucified.

### Question 2

Why should a Christian’s heart be overjoyed whenever he considers the doctrine of salvation? Talk about the meaning of atonement, justification, imputed righteousness, and sanctification. Why should these words set your heart ablaze and flood your soul with thanksgiving? Help: These answers are easy to find in Grudem’s 20 Christian Beliefs. If you google them, ask for Grudem’s definitions so they can be trusted.

### Question 3

Discuss the heartbreaking reality of verse 14. How does it inform the way you pray for the lost? How does it give you a sense of freedom when you share the gospel?

### Question 4

The last category, the fleshly Christian, has often been understood in unbiblical ways. Discuss what this verse does not mean and also what it does mean.

### Question 5
Take some time and have each group member consider which of the 3 categories they fit. If category one, let it affirm them. If category two, ask them to consider Christ and  heir need to become a Christian. If category three, then encourage them to repent and mature in their walk with the Lord.
