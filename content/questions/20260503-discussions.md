## Sermon

{{< collapse summary="**Sermon Summary**">}}

First Corinthians 3:10–17 frames the church as a building project founded only on Christ and energized by God’s grace. Paul portrays ministry leadership as the work of a skilled master builder who lays a foundation that no one may replace. The foundation must be Jesus Christ crucified, and every addition must align with that foundation. Ministry requires both the protective discipline of guarding the foundation from competing loyalties and the persistent labor of ongoing inspection and repair, because culture and time constantly threaten the church’s stability.

The text then shifts to the materials used in building: gold, silver, and precious stones versus wood, hay, and straw. Those materials do not simply represent activity levels; they reveal motive, craftsmanship, and eternal worth. On the day of the Lord a refining fire will disclose which works endure and which are consumed. Believers who face that fire will not lose salvation, but their works will stand or be burned; those whose labors endure receive reward, while what is burned results in loss though the person remains saved.

Paul also lays out practical tests for ministries. Every ministry must consistently make new disciples, help saints mature in holiness, fit the church’s character, and respond to discernment in prayer. Effective ministry arises where faithful interpretation of Scripture, honest appraisal of the congregation, and clear understanding of the surrounding culture intersect. Leaders must refuse appealing distractions that dilute Christ at the foundation and prefer projects that the foundation will bear.

Finally, the congregation itself functions as God’s temple: the Spirit dwells in the people, and any assault on the temple provokes God’s decisive opposition. That warning presses readers to examine personal priorities and communal ministries with eternal perspective. The charge points toward a church that pursues gospel purity, skillful leadership, and ministry shaped for lasting impact so that on the day of Christ the community displays gold, silver, and precious stones rather than ashes.

## Discussion Questions

### Question 1

Verses 10 and 11 talk about the healthy foundation of a church (hint: it is Jesus).
* What does it mean to have a foundation in Christ?
* How have you seen this at work in our church?

### Question 2

In Verse 10, Paul refers to himself as a 'skilled master builder' in relation to establishing the church in Corinth.
* Do you see our (Smyrna First) pastors through the same lense?
* How does that influence how you think about, support, and pray for your pastors?

### Question 3

Pastor Jeff talked about the need to ensure that the ministries that we develop have eternity in mind. Take some time to consider the way that you are a minister of the gospel (and yes, everyone has a ministry in one form or another).
* What aspects of your ministry are 'eternal' and should be strengthened and expanded?
* What aspects of your ministry are not eternal? How can they be change (or rebuilt) to be more eternally driven?

### Question 4

Paul tells us that on the final day, all of our work will be manifest and tested through the fire. Whatever is built on the foundation of Christ will survive into eternity. But whatever is not, will be burned up.
* What is your initial reaction to that idea?
* Why do you have the reaction that you do?
* How can you improve the way in which you build your life on the solid foundation of Christ?
