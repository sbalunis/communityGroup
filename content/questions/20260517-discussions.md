## Sermon

{{< collapse summary="**Sermon Summary**">}}

We gather around a single claim: the gospel rewires our inner life so that our motives line up with God’s truth. We confess that external actions can look fine while the heart runs on fear, self-promotion, or cold calculation. Scripture calls attention to the Corinthian fracture caused by loyalty to human leaders and public boasting, and it urges us to stop deceiving ourselves about the source of worth. Our minds carry the noetic effect of sin; our reasoning and moral judgments distort reality until Christ illumines them. The remedy arrives in the finished work of Christ: the ultimate verdict of our worth stands already declared in him, and all things in Christ belong to us as gift, not reward.

Because the verdict already stands, performance changes shape. We no longer perform to secure favor; we perform out of freedom, gratitude, and fidelity. That freedom reorients how we handle praise, criticism, and our own self-assessment. We must inspect motives now, not later, because the Lord will disclose hidden intentions when he returns and will give commendation accordingly. Healthy thinking begins by admitting our fallenness, receiving the gospel’s verdict, and letting that verdict govern our daily choices. When God shines light on our sin, we confess and repent so that present transformation can produce future commendation.

This summons brings urgent practicality. The gospel creates an urgency to address the internal places that corrode relationships and ministry, not to earn approval, but to live honestly under God’s scrutiny. A life shaped by the gospel looks like humble stewardship of God’s gifts, clear-eyed dependence on his grace, and consistent repentance when motives drift. We pursue a thought life that tracks God’s truth, so our outward actions flow from inward renewal and so we stand ready to receive the Lord’s commendation when he returns.

## Discussion Questions

### Question 1

Pastor Jeff reminded us that our motives often come from a place of fear, pride, or desire to impress rather than from a heart truly transformed by God’s grace.
* In what areas of your live are pure motives hard to come by?
* Can you give examples of how a good thing can be down with wrong motives?

### Question 2

Pastor Jeff shared how Paul sees pride and boasting as dangerous because they come from our sin nature and cause us to be deceived about our true worth.
* Why do you think Paul calls pride and boasting such serious sins?
* Why do you think people sometimes deceive themselves instead of facing God’s honest view of their lives?

### Question 3

Pastor Jeff used Madonna’s honest words about her endless struggle to prove her worth to show how we all can get stuck on a “gerbil wheel” of trying to earn value, which never satisfies.
* What can we learn from Madonna's admission?
* Why do you think trying to find worth by proving ourselves is like a broken cistern?

### Question 4

Pastor Jeff shared Tim Keller’s idea that the “ultimate verdict” is God’s final and unchangeable declaration that we are valuable and loved because of Christ. This flips our thinking because we don’t have to earn God’s approval; it’s already given.
* How does knowing this change the way you think about yourself and about your life?
* What does this mean for how we live? 

### Question 5

Pastor Jeff shared how Paul didn’t put much stock in what others thought of him. He (Paul) also though little of his own opinion of himself.
* How do we deal with the opinions that others have about us?
* What does Paul teach us about how we should view ourselves?

### Question 6

Pastor Jeff concluded that living for God’s praise alone frees us from the exhausting need to prove ourselves.
* How does this kind of life bring freedom and joy?
