## Discussion Questions

### Question 1

What do you think are the differences in the role of the church and the role of the government in handing disputes _between believers?_

_Note: 1 Corinthians involves civil disputes, while the governments also handles criminal offenses. (c.f. Romans 13:1-7 and 1 Peter 2:13-17)_

{{< collapse summary="**Romans 13:1-7**" >}}

^1^ Let every person be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been instituted by God. ^2^ Therefore whoever resists the authorities resists what God has appointed, and those who resist will incur judgment. ^3^ For rulers are not a terror to good conduct, but to bad. Would you have no fear of the one who is in authority? Then do what is good, and you will receive his approval, ^4^ for he is God's servant for your good. But if you do wrong, be afraid, for he does not bear the sword in vain. For he is the servant of God, an avenger who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer. ^5^ Therefore one must be in subjection, not only to avoid God's wrath but also for the sake of conscience. ^6^ For because of this you also pay taxes, for the authorities are ministers of God, attending to this very thing. ^7^ Pay to all what is owed to them: taxes to whom taxes are owed, revenue to whom revenue is owed, respect to whom respect is owed, honor to whom honor is owed.

### Question 2

How does true Christian love set the tone for how the church should handle disputes? How does our belief about the last things inform our disputes?

### Question 3

Why should we have confidence that we can solve disputes when they arise within the church? 

### Question 4

Paul references that the Christian has been washed, sanctified, and justified in the name of Christ Jesus.
* How does this impact our thinking and behavior as we week to settle our disputes?

### Question 5

Ken Sande offers the following helpful insights as to how we can apply the gospel to our disagreements. Which of these are most impactful to you?
* Remember the "Golden Result" _(that others will treat you how you have treated them)_
* Clarify the problem to eliminate misunderstanding.
* Use the gospel and not the law to settle our disagreements.
* Seek to diffuse the problem by using these helpful questions:
    1. How do you feel because of this conflict?
    2. What have you done to contribute to the problem?
    3. What do you think would please GOd as we work through this problem?
    4. What steps have already been taken to resolve this problem?
    5. What is the next right thing for us to do?
