> 
> ^4^ Therefore, as to the eating of food offered to idols, we know that “an idol has no real existence,” and that “there is no God but one.” ^5^ For although there may be so-called gods in heaven or on earth—as indeed there are many “gods” and many “lords”— ^6^ yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we exist.
>
> ^7^ However, not all possess this knowledge. But some, through former association with idols, eat food as really offered to an idol, and their conscience, being weak, is defiled. ^8^ Food will not commend us to God. We are no worse off if we do not eat, and no better off if we do. ^9^ But take care that this right of yours does not somehow become a stumbling block to the weak. ^10^ For if anyone sees you who have knowledge eating in an idol's temple, will he not be encouraged, if his conscience is weak, to eat food offered to idols? ^11^ And so by your knowledge this weak person is destroyed, the brother for whom Christ died. ^12^ Thus, sinning against your brothers and wounding their conscience when it is weak, you sin against Christ. ^13^ Therefore, if food makes my brother stumble, I will never eat meat, lest I make my brother stumble.
>

## Discussion Questions

### Question 1

Our culture makes it challenging us to stand for truth while loving others. Spirit-filled living provides the discernment to hold firm to biblical truth while remaining deeply sensitive to the consciences of those around us. 
- How does Spirit-filled living allow a believer to bring these important life principles together?

### Question 2

Pagan idolatry was everywhere in Corinth. "Knowers" felt free to eat meat because they knew idols weren't real, but this caused division with new converts who had previously worshiped those idols and found the association painful. 
- Discuss the significance of meat sacrificed to idols in this text and why it separated the “knowers” from the “weak.”

### Question 3

Applying the principles of 1 Corinthians 8 to modern ethical decisions.
- How do these principles inform your decision to attend a wedding of a couple pursuing a marriage that does not align with biblical marriage?

### Question 4

Knowledge without love leads to arrogance ("puffed up"). True knowledge of God leads to humility, realizing it is not about what we know, but that we are known by God.
- How can a person protect themselves from arrogance while growing in knowledge? How does knowledge deepen love?

### Question 5

Christian artist Shai Linne that right orthodoxy (doctrine) leads to right doxology (worship). Right doctrine (verses 4-6) establishes who God is, which naturally leads to deeper worship. Mature believers foster an environment where new believers can thrive by anchoring their lives in this gospel-centered truth.
- How does the truth in verses 4-6 help new believers thrive, and how does a mature believer contribute to this culture?

### Question 6

A conscience is an internal faculty that distinguishes right from wrong. Insisting on personal freedom at the expense of a young believer is unwise, as it can damage their conscience and draw them back into harmful lifestyles.
- Why is it unwise to insist on expressing Christian freedom at the expense of a young believer’s conscience?

### Question 7

Sinning against a brother’s conscience is, in effect, sinning against Christ. Choosing to abstain from personal liberties (like social drinking, if it causes others to stumble) prioritizes their spiritual well-being over our own desires.
- If causing a person to struggle is a sin, how is God calling you to apply verse 13 to your own life?

### Question 8

Pastor Jeff reminded us that God is the giver of every good gift, even when He gives us gifts we might not have chosen for ourselves. When you face a season where God’s plan feels different from your desires, how do you practically turn back to Scripture—like Matthew 6:8 or James 1:17—to rebuild your trust in His goodness?
