> ^8^ Do I say these things on human authority? Does not the Law say the same? ^9^ For it is written in the Law of Moses, “You shall not muzzle an ox when it treads out the grain.” Is it for oxen that God is concerned? ^10^ Does he not certainly speak for our sake? Nevertheless, we have not made use of this right, but we endure anything rather than put an obstacle in the way of the gospel of Christ.
> 
> ^13^ Do you not know that those who are employed in the temple service get their food from the temple, and those who serve at the altar share in the sacrificial offerings? ^14^ In the same way, the Lord commanded that those who proclaim the gospel should get their living by the gospel.
> 
> ^15^ But I have made no use of any of these rights, nor am I writing these things to secure any such provision. For I would rather die than have anyone deprive me of my ground for boasting. ^16^ For if I preach the gospel, that gives me no ground for boasting. For necessity is laid upon me. Woe to me if I do not preach the gospel! ^17^ For if I do this of my own will, I have a reward, but if not of my own will, I am still entrusted with a stewardship. ^18^ What then is my reward? That in my preaching I may present the gospel free of charge, so as not to make full use of my right in the gospel.
> 
> ^19^ For though I am free from all, I have made myself a servant to all, that I might win more of them. ^20^ To the Jews I became as a Jew, in order to win Jews. To those under the law I became as one under the law (though not being myself under the law) that I might win those under the law. ^21^ To those outside the law I became as one outside the law (not being outside the law of God but under the law of Christ) that I might win those outside the law. ^22^ To the weak I became weak, that I might win the weak. I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some. ^23^ I do it all for the sake of the gospel, that I may share with them in its blessings.
> 
> ^24^ Do you not know that in a race all the runners run, but only one receives the prize? So run that you may obtain it. ^25^ Every athlete exercises self-control in all things. ## Discussion Questions

Take some time and talk about the role of pastor and deacon within our church. Pray, in particular, for our newly ordained deacons, Scott Allen, Saindon Balunis, Andrew Cooper, and Sean Murray. Ask the Lord to give them strength and fortitude as they serve our church in such an important time in our church’s history.

### Question 1

Paul emphasizes that true discipleship involves self-denial, yet the Psalmist speaks of "fullness of joy" in God’s presence. How do we reconcile the call to suffer and surrender with the deep, soul-satisfying joy of living for Jesus?

### Question 2

Paul emphasizes that true discipleship involves self-denial, yet the Psalmist speaks of "fullness of joy" in God’s presence. How do we reconcile the call to suffer and surrender with the deep, soul-satisfying joy of living for Jesus?

### Question 3

Paul had legitimate rights as an apostle (like being supported materially or having companionship), yet he willingly laid them aside to ensure nothing hindered the Gospel. Which of his choices surprise you most? What does his example teach us about the priority of the Gospel over our own personal comforts?

### Question 4

Paul explains that his true reward wasn't material comfort, but the sheer privilege of preaching the Gospel free of charge. How does this perspective change how you view your daily responsibilities? If you treated sharing the Gospel as your greatest reward, how would your daily priorities shift?

### Question 5

What does it actually mean to be "all things to all people" without compromising our values? How does this reflect the *Incarnation*—Jesus meeting us where we are? Discuss what it looks like to be sensitive to the world around us for the sake of "winning some" to Christ.

### Question 6

Paul uses sports metaphors (like the runner or boxer) to explain the urgency of spiritual discipline. As you think about the prayer of preparation—*“Lord, do everything you need to do in me so that You can do everything You want to do through me”*—what is one area where you need God to work in you right now to prepare you for future ministry?
