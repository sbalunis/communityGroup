## Discussion Questions

*Saints Becoming Saints: The Lord's Supper, Part 2*

**Big Idea:** Our church must align our observance of the Lord's Supper with the gospel's account of the Last Supper.

- Contrast what Jesus did with what we actually do.
- Repent of the selfishness that we'd rather not change.
- Enjoy the benefits of deepening fellowship. 

{{< collapse summary="**Text: 1 Corinthians 11:23-34**" >}}

> For I received from the Lord what I also delivered to you, that the Lord Jesus on the night when he was betrayed took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it, and said, “This is my body, which is for you. Do this in remembrance of me.” In the same way also he took the cup, after supper, saying, “This cup is the new covenant in my blood. Do this, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of me.” For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes.
> 
> Whoever, therefore, eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be guilty concerning the body and blood of the Lord. Let a person examine himself, then, and so eat of the bread and drink of the cup. For anyone who eats and drinks without discerning the body eats and drinks judgment on himself. That is why many of you are weak and ill, and some have died. But if we judged ourselves truly, we would not be judged. But when we are judged by the Lord, we are disciplined so that we may not be condemned along with the world.
> 
> So then, my brothers, when you come together to eat, wait for one another— if anyone is hungry, let him eat at home—so that when you come together it will not be for judgment. About the other things I will give directions when I come.

> *English Standard Version (ESV)*

{{< collapse summary="**Sermon Summary**" >}}

Paul confronts a church that had turned the Lord’s Supper into something it was never meant to be. The Corinthians came together with divisions, factions, selfishness, and humiliation of those who had little. The table that should have proclaimed Christ had become a place where people looked out for themselves. Paul will not commend that, because coming together in that way is not for the better but for the worse.

The Lord’s table centers everything on Jesus. Jesus took bread on the night He was given over, and the Father gave over His Son in love so that sinners could be made right with Him. The bread points to Christ’s body, sacrificially given for all who believe. The cup points to the new covenant in His blood, the covenant promised through Jeremiah, where forgiveness comes through knowing the Lord. Christ fulfills what every former sacrifice only pointed toward.

The table calls the church to remember Christ, give thanks to Christ, and proclaim Christ’s death until He comes. The bread and cup cannot become about personal preference, private experience, or meeting physical hunger. The little wafer will not satisfy physical hunger, because the table points to the deeper hunger of the heart. Christ alone provides what His people need forever.

Paul’s warning about taking the Supper in an unworthy manner does not mean that only sinless people may come. No sinner possesses worthiness in himself. Christ’s finished work makes it possible for those who trust Him to come in a worthy manner. Paul calls each person to examine himself, to be honest about sin, and to discern the body.

The examination reaches both upward and outward. The heart must ask whether there is sin being held onto rather than surrendered to the Lord. The heart must also ask whether relationships at home, in the church, with family, and with neighbors are marked by obedience and peace. Repentance becomes hard when sin is something a person does not want to let go of, but God calls His children to deal with it rather than resist Him.

God’s discipline is serious, but it is still grace. Paul says it is better to judge sin truly than to force the Lord to bring discipline. The church should welcome His correction as the loving provision that keeps His people from being condemned with the world. The table therefore calls believers to wait for one another, welcome one another, and put the needs of the body before their own.

Christ’s death anchors the past, sustains the present, and secures the future. Jesus has brought His people from death to life. Jesus now holds them fast through every sorrow. Jesus will return, and until that day His church proclaims that He is risen indeed.

### Observation questions

1. What specific problems were happening when the Corinthian church gathered for the Lord’s Supper, and how were people with little being affected?
2. According to Jeremiah 31 and Jesus’ words in Luke 22, what does the new covenant provide, and what is the cup meant to represent?

### Interpretation Questions

3. Why does Paul treat the Corinthians’ selfishness and divisions at the Lord’s Table as a serious problem rather than merely poor manners? What does their behavior communicate about Christ and His church?
4. What is the difference between trying to become personally worthy enough to come to the table and coming in a worthy manner because of Christ’s finished work?
5. Why is the Lord’s discipline described as grace rather than condemnation for those who belong to Christ?

### Application Questions

6. The Lord’s Table is about Christ rather than personal preference, private experience, or having one’s own needs met. What attitudes or expectations can quietly make church gatherings more about self than about Christ? 
7. Paul calls the church to wait for one another and put the needs of others first. How can our group and church become more intentional about noticing those who may feel overlooked, excluded, or in need of care? 
8.  When life brings sorrow, fear, or uncertainty, where are you most tempted to look for stability? How does Christ’s death, His present care, and His promised return give you a stronger hope? 
9.  What does it look like in everyday life to hunger for Christ rather than for things that draw your heart away from Him? What practice could help you remember your deeper need for Him this week?
