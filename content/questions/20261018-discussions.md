## Discussion Questions

*Saints Becoming Saints: Spiritual Gifts*

{{< collapse summary="**Text: 1 Corinthians 12:1-11**" >}}

>Now concerning spiritual gifts, brothers, I do not want you to be uninformed. You know that when you were pagans you were led astray to mute idols, however you were led. Therefore I want you to understand that no one speaking in the Spirit of God ever says “Jesus is accursed!” and no one can say “Jesus is Lord” except in the Holy Spirit.
>
>Now there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are varieties of service, but the same Lord; and there are varieties of activities, but it is the same God who empowers them all in everyone. To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good. For to one is given through the Spirit the utterance of wisdom, and to another the utterance of knowledge according to the same Spirit, to another faith by the same Spirit, to another gifts of healing by the one Spirit, to another the working of miracles, to another prophecy, to another the ability to distinguish between spirits, to another various kinds of tongues, to another the interpretation of tongues. All these are empowered by one and the same Spirit, who apportions to each one individually as he wills.
>
> *English Standard Version (ESV)*

{{< /collapse >}}

{{< collapse summary="**Sermon Summary**" >}}

Paul’s picture of the church begins like a team pulling on one rope. Every person matters, every person is needed, and nobody gets to stand off to the side while others carry the weight. The body of Christ is one body with many members, and each member brings God given abilities for the good of the whole church.

Paul says believers should not be unaware of spiritual gifts. The Holy Spirit puts power within every believer, while mute idols have no power at all. Jesus is Lord, and that confession itself comes through the Holy Spirit. The Spirit reveals Christ, awakens believers to sin, guides behavior, produces spiritual fruit, and gives gifts for serving.

Spiritual gifts come in varieties, but the same Spirit gives them. Ministries come in varieties, but the same Lord provides opportunities to serve. Effects and activities come in varieties, but the same God energizes His people for the work He has called them to do. God’s gifts are not given so a person can build a platform, but so the church can be unified, strengthened, and built up for the common good.

Ephesians 2:10 says believers are God’s workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works prepared beforehand. A vocation may provide for a family, but a spiritual calling uses God given gifts to fulfill the good works God has already prepared. God has placed a unique fingerprint on every believer, and that fingerprint includes spiritual gifts, strengths, and places of service.

Paul names gifts such as words of wisdom, words of knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discernment, tongues, and interpretation of tongues. The word of wisdom brings God’s Word into a situation in the right way and at the right moment. The word of knowledge receives insight that God reveals. The gift of faith stands steady when everyone else is trembling and says, “God is able.”

The gifts of healing and miracles show that God is still able to work as He sees fit. Prophecy primarily declares God’s Word and makes known His truth. Discernment helps the church see not only what is good, but what is best, exposing doctrinal forgeries that can mislead God’s people. Tongues and interpretation serve the goal of making God’s truth understandable.

The one and same Spirit distributes gifts individually just as He wills. God chooses the gifts, God supplies the power, and God calls His people to discover His plan and serve where He has placed them. Jesus also calls those who have never believed to turn from sin and receive the gift of salvation.

{{< /collapse >}}

1.  Paul uses the human body to describe the church. How does this analogy change your perspective on your own role and value within our church family?
2.  How would you describe the difference between a natural talent or vocation and a God-given spiritual gift?
3.  We are told that the same Holy Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead works in us. How does knowing that this power is available to you change how you approach serving?
4.  The sermon emphasizes that we are God's "workmanship" (Ephesians 2:10) created for good works prepared beforehand. How can you practically seek to discover the unique fingerprint or gifts God has placed on your life, while avoiding the pride of comparing your gifts to others?
5.  Considering the local church body, how does the recognition of spiritual gifts—regardless of how "visible" or "hidden" they may be—change the way you serve your brothers and sisters in our congregation?
